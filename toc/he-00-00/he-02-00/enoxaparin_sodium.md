| Dosing             | ●Ischemic heart disease: Acute coronary syndromes: -Non-ST-elevation acute coronary syndromes: SUBQ: 1 mg/kg every 12 hours in conjunction with an appropriate antiplatelet regimen; continue for at least 48 hours, until hospital discharge, or until percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is performed. -ST-elevation myocardial infarction: Patients <75 years of age: Single IV bolus of 30 mg plus 1 mg/kg (maximum: 100 mg for the first 2 doses only) SUBQ every 12 hours. The first SUBQ dose should be administered with the IV bolus. Patients ?75 years of age: Note: No IV bolus is administered. SUBQ: 0.75 mg/kg (maximum: 75 mg for the first 2 doses only) every 12 hours. Duration: Therapy may be continued for up to 8 days (minimum of 48 hours when undergoing reperfusion with fibrinolysis) or until revascularization. ●Venous thromboembolism, prophylaxis: -Medical patients with acute illness at moderate and high risk for venous thromboembolism: SUBQ: 40 mg once daily; continue for length of hospital stay or until patient is fully ambulatory and risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE) has diminished. -Pregnancy (off label): Low dose: <100 kg: SUBQ: 40 mg once daily >=100 kg: SUBQ: 60 mg once daily. Intermediate dose (fixed): SUBQ: 40 mg every 12 hours or Intermediate dose (weight-adjusted): <50 kg: SUBQ: 60 mg once daily. 50 to <70 kg: SUBQ: 80 mg once daily. 70 to <100 kg: SUBQ: 100 mg once daily. >=100 kg: SUBQ: 120 mg once daily. -Total hip arthroplasty or hip fracture surgery: Total hip arthroplasty or hip fracture surgery: SUBQ: 40 mg once daily or 30 mg every 12 hours, with initial dose administered ?12 hours preoperatively or ?12 hours postoperatively once hemostasis is achieved (Ref). Optimal duration of prophylaxis is unknown, but it is usually given for a minimum of 10 to 14 days and can be extended for up to 35 days. -Total knee arthroplasty: SUBQ: 30 mg every 12 hours or 40 mg once daily, with initial dose administered ?12 hours preoperatively or ?12 hours postoperatively once hemostasis is achieved. Optimal duration of prophylaxis is unknown, but it is usually given for a minimum of 10 to 14 days and can be extended for up to 35 days in patients who are not fully ambulatory. ●Venous thromboembolism, treatment -Deep vein thrombosis: SUBQ: 1 mg/kg every 12 hours (preferred) or 1.5 mg/kg once every 24 hours. Provoked venous thromboembolism with a transient risk factor: 3 months with reassessment for resolution of the provoking factor. Unprovoked venous thromboembolism or provoked venous thromboembolism with a persistent risk factor: >=3 months depending on risk of VTE recurrence and bleeding. -Venous thromboembolism treatment in pregnancy: SUBQ: 1 mg/kg every 12 hours. Prior to delivery: Anticoagulation management is individualized. Options include replacing with UFH at ~36 weeks' gestation or continuing until ~39 weeks' gestation in patients at very low risk of delivery. Discontinue enoxaparin ?24 hours before delivery, particularly if neuraxial anesthesia is planned. Postpartum: May restart ?4 to 6 hours after vaginal delivery or ?6 to 12 hours after cesarean delivery, unless significant bleeding occurred or neuraxial anesthesia was used. Optimal duration of anticoagulation is unknown. In general, total duration (antepartum plus postpartum) should be at least 3 to 6 months with at least 6 weeks postpartum, but potentially longer. |

| Contraindications  | Contraindicated in patients allergic to enoxaparin, heparin, or related products (including other low molecular weight heparins). Also contraindicated in those with a history of immune-mediated heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) within the past 100 days or with circulating antibodies. Do not use in patients with active bleeding, bleeding disorders unrelated to heparin (except for DIC in some cases), bleeding-prone organ lesions, or intracranial hemorrhage. Not recommended in patients with severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance ~30 mL/min by Cockcroft’s formula), except those on dialysis. Unfractionated heparin is preferred for these patients. Spinal or epidural anesthesia is contraindicated during low molecular weight heparin therapy.
