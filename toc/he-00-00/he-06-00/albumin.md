| Dosing             | Hypovolemic Shock: The dosage and infusion rate must be individualized based on the patient's response. The total dose should not exceed the level of albumin found in a normal individual (approximately 2 g/kg of body weight in non-bleeding circumstances). --- Burns: The treatment goal should be to maintain a plasma albumin concentration in the range of 2.5 +/- 0.5 g/100 mL, with a plasma colloid osmotic pressure of 20 mm Hg (equivalent to a total plasma protein concentration of 5.2 g/100 mL) --- Hypoproteinemia With or Without Edema: The usual daily dose of albumin is 50 to 75 g for adults and 25 g for children; patients with severe hypoproteinemia who continue to lose albumin may require higher doses. --- Cirrhotic ascites, therapeutic large volume paracentesis (adjunctive agent): High concentration (eg, 20% to 25% albumin): IV: 6 to 8 g for every liter removed or 50 g total for paracentesis >5 L. Note: Administer at the time of or soon after the procedure to avoid paracentesis complications (eg, hypovolemia, hyponatremia, kidney impairment). --- Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome, severe (treatment) (25% solution only): 25% albumin: IV: 50 to 100 g over 4 hours; repeat at 4- to 12-hour intervals as needed. --- Plasma exchange, therapeutic: Low concentration (eg, 4% to 5% albumin): IV: Titrate dose to plasma volume removed during procedure. |
